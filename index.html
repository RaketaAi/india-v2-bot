india-v2-bot

An automated client that requests published documents from Indian financial market publishers. If it has reached your site, this page is what it is.

What it sends

india-v2-bot/1.0 (+https://raketaai.github.io/india-v2-bot/)

That is the identity it sends everywhere but at two publishers, and everywhere else it never asserts a Referer header.

The two exceptions are the National Stock Exchange of India and BSE. Both refuse any client that does not present as a web browser: measured on 23 September 2026, BSE refused this name on every request and NSE left every connection unanswered. To those two alone the program sends the headers of a Chrome browser, with the Referer of the exchange's own site, and to BSE also the Origin of BSE's own site. Nothing else is added. It asks them only for filings the Listing Regulations make public, it honours their robots.txt — including a rule that names india-v2-bot, though it does not send that name there — and every such request is recorded against this exception. If either exchange would rather admit the program under its own name, write to the address below and it will switch.

What it is

It collects published documents — issuers' financial results, annual reports and regulatory disclosures, credit rating agencies' rating rationales, and the lists of filings and ratings that index them — from exchanges, regulators, credit rating agencies and issuers in India. They feed a private research platform on the issuers of the rupee bond market. Nothing collected is redistributed, resold, licensed or served to anyone.

It is automated, and its code is written by AI coding agents (Anthropic's Claude), under the operator's direction and review. That is disclosed here rather than compressed into a token, and it is not a claim to act for Anthropic: this program is not one of their crawlers and does not send their crawler names.

How it behaves

  • It honours your robots.txt, including where doing so costs it documents it wants. It follows RFC 9309: a rule naming india-v2-bot is obeyed wherever it is written, and where no rule names it, the rules for every robot apply. If your robots.txt is missing or refused, nothing is disallowed; if it does not answer, the program asks for nothing on your site until it does.
  • It asks for specific things: an issuer's list of filings or ratings, found through the site's own search, and the documents that list names. To learn how a site lists them it may fetch the site's front page and scripts once. It does not follow links to see what a site holds.
  • It is slow on purpose: at most one request every two seconds to any host.
  • It never creates an account, registers, accepts terms, asserts a membership, or pays for anything — in anyone's name, including the operator's. A source behind a login or a membership is recorded as not asked, and left so.
  • It does not disguise itself, except as stated above. Anywhere else, if you refuse this name, it is refused: the refusal is written down and the source is done without, rather than worked around.

How to make it stop

Refuse it in your robots.txt and it will stop. It reads the file and obeys it:

User-agent: india-v2-bot
Disallow: /

Or say so directly — write to contact@raketa.ai, or open an issue here. Name the host and, if you have it, a log line. It will be stopped, and no reason is needed.
